2. Business concepts that must remain separate
Customer selling price is what the customer owes for the agreed service. Supplier/final cost is the economic cost attributed to that service under the owner’s method. Supplier cash/card funding is money charged to a payment method. Voucher/travel-credit funding is a different form of payment. Customer receipts are actual incoming payments. Allocations assign receipts or approved customer credit to particular bills. Booking margin, expected commission, received commission, cash movement and net business profit are different measures. For referral deals, supplier commission owed TO Padova and referral remuneration owed BY Padova must remain distinct, with separate amounts, conditions and payment histories. A hotel providing the accommodation, the person/platform providing its rate, the customer/payer, traveler and referrer can be different parties. Preserve these distinctions; do not assume they always are different or that every booking needs every party. For purchased credits, acquisition cost and face/usable value are additional distinct amounts. Track the seller and source account separately from the hotel or airline providing travel. Do not assume booking value equals either Padova’s selling price or its cash acquisition cost. The hotel’s regular/reference price is also a separate benchmark: in the clarified example regular price $1,000, Padova cost $700 and selling price $850 produce $150 booking margin before other expenses. A reduction from a published hotel price is not automatically Padova’s earned margin.

SPECIFIC CAPITAL ONE → VOUCHERIZATION WORKFLOW: example confirmed by owner: Padova has a $1,000 booking. Capital One price matching generates $400 of Padova travel credit, so the original booking can show +$400 price-match profit/value under the already-settled rule. Padova may later voucherize that ticket/booking into a Padova-owned voucher with $1,000 face value. The preferred design is NOT to force the voucher onto the same accounting line as the original booking. Instead, create a separate Voucher Asset record with its own ID and ledger, permanently linked back to the source booking and related price-match-credit events for traceability. Voucher creation itself is an asset conversion of the original ticket/booking value, NOT another $1,000 of profit.


To prevent double counting, the voucher ledger must keep face value / usable value separate from accounting/source carrying value. Under the already-confirmed rule that the $400 price-match credit is profit when earned, the original booking can remain at +$400 profit even after voucherization. The new $1,000 voucher should then carry $1,000 of source value for later gain/loss measurement. If Padova sells that $1,000 voucher for $900, the voucher transaction records a -$100 realized loss/discount. The original booking still shows +$400; the voucher sale shows -$100; combined economics are therefore +$300 before other costs. If Padova instead uses the $1,000 voucher at full face value on a new booking, the voucher event itself produces $0 additional gain/loss: the voucher simply supplies $1,000 of real funding to that new booking. Any profit on the new booking comes from that booking's own sale price versus its $1,000 voucher-funded cost. This separate-record approach is preferred because vouchers can later be used for unrelated customers/bookings.


If Padova sells or uses only part of a voucher, reduce the voucher balance and allocate the $1,000 source carrying value proportionally to the face value consumed/sold unless an issuer-specific rule requires another method. Voucher sale/use transactions may therefore appear in a separate voucher ledger or monthly adjustment activity rather than cluttering the original booking line, while still retaining a source-booking link. If Padova purchases a voucher from a third party, preserve both face value and acquisition cost and track later use/resale against that acquisition cost. The system must be able to show the full chain when needed: source booking → price-match credits earned → voucher created → voucher uses/sales → remaining balance → final realized gain/loss. Exact historical classification of ambiguous voucher rows must be reviewed during migration rather than guessed. Existing voucher/source sheets remain READ ONLY.
